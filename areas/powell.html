Powell Plumbing, Block by Block

Beaver Creek runs right through Powell, and after a hard East Tennessee rain the creek tells you which sewer lines are compromised: low-lying homes near the creek see backups and gurgling drains when groundwater pushes into cracked laterals. The ranch homes built here in the 1960s through the 1980s are hitting the age where copper supply lines pinhole and cast-iron drains scale up and narrow — a camera inspection usually answers in ten minutes what a week of plunging can't.

The newer subdivisions off Beaver Creek Drive and along Clinton Highway bring a different list: builder-grade fixtures failing early, tankless water heaters that never got their annual descaling, and water heaters parked in unheated garages that give up during the first real cold snap of January. Either way, you get a real arrival window and a price you approve before work starts.

Powell also leans DIY — this is a community that fixes things — so a lot of our calls start with "I tried the snake already." That's fine. A pro with a camera finds the real cause instead of clearing the same clog for the fourth time, and you get a straight answer about whether it's a $200 clearing or a line that's finally done.
