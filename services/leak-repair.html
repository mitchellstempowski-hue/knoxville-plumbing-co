Leak Repair Options in Knoxville

Spot Pipe Repair

One leak, one targeted fix. The damaged section is cut out and replaced — the right call when the rest of the line is healthy.

Slab Leak Repair

A small opening directly over the leak lets the pro repair it in place, then patch the slab — no trenching across your floor.

Pipe Rerouting

The leaking under-slab line is abandoned and a new line runs through walls or the attic instead. Best for older pipes that keep failing.

Joint & Fitting Repair

Drips under sinks, at water heater connections, and behind fixtures — resealed or replaced before they rot out cabinets and subfloors.

Water Line Repair

Leaking main lines between the meter and the house: repaired in sections or replaced, with the yard put back the way it was found.

Repiping

When leaks keep coming back, partial or whole-home repiping with PEX ends the cycle — quoted as one flat project, not leak by leak.

Not sure where the water is coming from yet? Start with leak detection — pinpointing the leak first keeps the repair opening small and the quote honest.

Local know-how

Why the Right Repair Matters in Knoxville Homes

What leaks in Knoxville depends on the house. The older homes in neighborhoods like Sequoyah Hills and Fourth & Gill still run galvanized and aging copper that pinholes from the inside out — patch one spot and the next weak joint is already thinning. The newer slab homes in West Knoxville and Farragut have the opposite pattern: sound pipe stressed by clay-and-shale soil that swells in wet springs and shrinks in dry summers, flexing lines until a joint or bend gives way. That's why a good Knoxville plumber talks options, not just price: a spot repair on a healthy line can last decades, while the same repair on a corroded system buys you a few months. Rerouting or repiping costs more once — and ends the leak-of-the-month cycle for good.

FAQ

Leak Repair FAQ

Most accessible pipe and joint leak repairs in Knoxville run $200–$600. Slab leak repair typically runs $800–$2,500 depending on access, and a reroute is quoted as a flat project price. You approve the full price before any work starts.

A single leak in an otherwise healthy line is usually a spot repair. If the pipe is older, corroded, or has leaked before, rerouting the line through the walls or attic avoids the slab entirely and is usually the smarter long-term fix.

A spot repair needs one small, targeted opening directly at the leak — not a trench across the room. A reroute avoids opening the floor at all. Your pro will show you both options and the price of each before cutting anything.

Policies often cover the water damage a leak causes, but usually not the cost of repairing the pipe itself. Your plumber can document the leak's location and cause, which helps if you file a claim for the damage.
